# all_types_of_words.md

Words in English are grouped into categories called parts of speech or word classes based on how they function in a
sentence. They are generally divided into open classes (which constantly add new words) and closed classes (which have a
fixed set of words).

The Major Word Classes (Open Classes)

These categories contain thousands of members and grow over time as the language evolves:
• Nouns: Name people, places, things, or ideas (e.g., dog, London, happiness). They can be broken down into further
types, such as common, proper, countable, uncountable, abstract, and collective.
• Verbs: Express actions, occurrences, or states of being (e.g., run, happen, is). A sentence cannot exist without a
verb.
• Adjectives: Modify or describe nouns and pronouns to make their meaning more precise (e.g., big, blue, clever).
• Adverbs: Modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs, often telling how, when, where, or to what degree (e.g., quickly,
very, yesterday).

The Minor Word Classes (Closed Classes)

These functional categories have a fixed or limited number of members that connect and structure the open-class words:
• Pronouns: Replace nouns to make sentences shorter and clearer (e.g., he, she, they, who).
• Prepositions: Show spatial, temporal, or logical relationships between a noun/pronoun and other words (e.g., in, on,
under, to).
• Conjunctions: Connect words, phrases, or clauses together (e.g., and, but, because, or).
• Determiners: Introduce nouns and provide context like quantity or possession (e.g., the, a, this, some).
• Interjections: Express sudden emotions or reactions (e.g., ouch, oh, wow).
You can learn more about how words function using the Cambridge Dictionary Grammar Guide or review the EnglishClub Word
